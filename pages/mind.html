THE MIND

How I think.

Curiosity is one of the strongest forces behind the way I learn, question, and understand things.

HOW I APPROACH THE WORLD

I want to understand how things work.

I naturally want to understand systems rather than simply use them. When something interests me, I tend to look beneath the surface and ask how the pieces connect.

I enjoy learning through experimentation. Building something, breaking it, finding the problem, and understanding why it failed often teaches me more than simply reading an explanation.

I am interested in programming, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, automation, technology, philosophy, human behavior, and systems in general.

I also tend to question assumptions. Something being commonly believed does not automatically make it correct, and something being complicated does not mean it cannot be understood.

At the same time, curiosity can become a weakness. I can become interested in too many things at once, start more projects than I can finish, and overcomplicate problems that could have been solved more simply.

Part of becoming better is learning to turn curiosity into consistent action.

HOW I LEARN

Curiosity becomes useful when it becomes practice.

01

Question

I start by asking why something works, why it fails, or whether there is another way.

02

Research

I collect information and try to understand the underlying concepts rather than memorizing isolated answers.

03

Experiment

I learn by testing ideas and seeing what happens when theory meets reality.

04

Build

Creating something turns abstract knowledge into something I can actually use.

05

Break

Mistakes expose gaps in understanding and give me something concrete to investigate.

06

Improve

Understanding becomes valuable when I can use it to build something better the next time.
